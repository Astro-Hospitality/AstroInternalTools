Module 1 of 5
Bribery & Corruption

What is bribery?

Offering, giving, receiving, or asking for something of value to influence a decision or action in your favour. It doesn't have to be cash — it can be gifts, hospitality, discounts, or favours.

What is corruption?

Misusing your position or authority for personal gain. This includes kickbacks, inflated contracts with preferred suppliers, or using Astro's resources for private benefit.

Real-world scenarios

Red Flag

A supplier offers you a case of wine "just as a thank you" right before contract renewal time. Accepting this gift could compromise — or appear to compromise — your objectivity. Decline politely and report it to your manager.

Acceptable

A supplier brings muffins to a team meeting. A small, infrequent, openly shared gift with no conditions attached is unlikely to create a conflict — but always use your judgement and check with your manager if unsure.

The Astro rule: Never offer or accept anything of value that is intended to — or could be perceived to — influence a business decision. When in doubt, say no and tell someone.

Module 2 of 5
Fraud Awareness

Internal Fraud

  • Falsifying expense claims
  • Approving fictitious invoices
  • Timesheet manipulation
  • Misuse of company funds

External Fraud

  • Phishing emails or fake suppliers
  • Invoice fraud / payment redirect
  • Identity theft
  • Cybercrime targeting business accounts

Warning Signs

  • Unusual payment requests
  • Pressure to bypass approvals
  • Unexplained financial discrepancies
  • New banking details from known suppliers
1
Always verify bank account changes directly with a known contact at the supplier — never rely on details sent via email alone.
2
Follow approval processes. Requests to skip sign-off "just this once" are a common fraud tactic. No exception is worth the risk.
3
Protect your login credentials. Never share passwords. If you suspect your account has been compromised, report it to IT immediately.
Red Flag

You receive an email from a regular supplier saying they've changed their bank account and need payment sent to a new number urgently. Stop. Call the supplier on a known number to verify before processing anything.

Module 3 of 5
Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest happens when your personal interests — financial, relational, or otherwise — could interfere with your ability to make fair, objective decisions on behalf of Astro. Having a conflict isn't automatically wrong — failing to disclose it is.

Common examples

  • Approving a supplier in which you hold a personal financial interest
  • Having a financial stake in a competing or partner business
  • Making purchasing decisions that personally benefit you financially
  • Accepting a secondary role or consultancy with a supplier without disclosure

What to do

  • Disclose any potential conflict to your manager immediately
  • Remove yourself from the relevant decision or process
  • Document the disclosure
  • When in doubt — disclose. It's always better to raise it and have it cleared than to stay silent
Red Flag

You're involved in selecting a new linen supplier and you realise you hold shares in one of the shortlisted companies. Disclose this immediately to your manager and step aside from the evaluation process — even if you believe your judgement wouldn't be affected.

Module 4 of 5
Money Laundering

What is money laundering?

Making illegally obtained money appear legitimate — typically by passing it through a legitimate business. Even unknowingly facilitating this can expose Astro and its people to serious legal consequences.

Red flags to watch for

  • Customers paying unusually large amounts in cash
  • Requests for payments to third parties unrelated to the transaction
  • Overpayments followed by refund requests to a different account
  • Customers unwilling to provide standard business information
  • Transactions that make no obvious commercial sense

Your responsibilities

  • Know your customers and suppliers — basic due diligence is everyone's job
  • Be alert to unusual or complex payment arrangements
  • Never process a transaction you're uncomfortable with without raising it first
  • Report suspicious activity to your manager or Finance immediately

Important: If you suspect money laundering, do not alert the customer or supplier — this is called "tipping off" and may itself be illegal. Report internally first and let management guide next steps.

Module 5 of 5
Speak Up — How to Report a Concern

Astro is built on trust — between our team, our partners, and the hotels we serve. If something doesn't feel right, say something. Raising a concern is always the right call, and you will never be penalised for doing so in good faith.

1
Talk to your manager — in most cases, this is the fastest and most direct route to resolving a concern.
2
Go to senior leadership — if you're not comfortable speaking to your direct manager, escalate to a senior leader or director.
3
Contact Reuben Beatson, Managing Director — for serious or sensitive matters, you can reach out confidentially at reuben@astro.net.nz.

You are protected

Any employee who raises a concern in good faith is protected from retaliation. This includes concerns that turn out to be unfounded, provided they were raised honestly.

Keep records

If you witness something suspicious, note the date, time, people involved, and what you observed. Detailed records help investigations significantly.
